# Keys, good and bad

| Subject | Weak keys | Better keys | Why |
| - | - | - | - |
| A lighthouse called the Saltmarsh Light | light, tower | Saltmarsh Light, the lighthouse, the lamp room | "light" appears in ordinary sentences constantly |
| A secret society | society, secret | the Ninefold, Ninefold, the ninth door | abstract words fire everywhere or never |
| Maren's dead brother | brother, him | Tobias, Toby, Maren's brother | pronouns and relations are too common |
| A cursed bell | bell | drowned bell, the bell under the pier | narrow a common noun with the phrase people use |
| A town | town, village | Greyhollow, the harbour town | use the proper name and how locals say it |
| A magic system | magic | tidecraft, tide-magic, tidecaller | name the thing the story calls it |

## Secondary keys

A primary key "bell" plus a secondary key "pier" or "tide" fires the bell entry
only when the conversation is about *that* bell — not a doorbell, not a church
bell in another scene.

## Variants to include

- Short and long forms: "the Ninefold", "Ninefold".
- Possessives and plurals where people use them: "marsh", "marshes".
- Nicknames the story uses: "Toby" for Tobias.
- Misspellings are rarely worth a key; a name the character repeats will be
  typed correctly soon enough.
